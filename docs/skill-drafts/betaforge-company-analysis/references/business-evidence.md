# Business evidence for the investment philosophy

Draft working definitions for the [adopted philosophy](../../../analysis-policy.md#investment-philosophy). Use them to make judgments inspectable, without a universal score or numerical threshold. The [calculation guide](calculations.md) owns the simple financial diagnostics. These examples illustrate questions to investigate; they are not facts about any issuer or default forecast assumptions.

## Write the business brief before forecasting

Start with preserved issuer reports and comparable company history. In a short private brief, explain what the company sells, who buys it and why customers choose it. Select three to five developments that could materially change earnings, cash needs or per-share value. For each development, record:

- What happened, with the dated source and the activity it affects.
- How it could affect earnings or cash, with evidence for the likely size and timing and any material unknowns.
- The strongest contrary evidence or an explicit gap, and the next observation that would test the claim.
- What it changes in a forecast assumption or sensitivity, or why it stays as context.

Before freezing evidence for a new run, the coordinator can use this outline to identify up to three questions that could change a case or the investment call. Use the [market and peer research guide](../../betaforge-market-peer-research/SKILL.md#2-connect-market-evidence-to-operating-drivers) for any manual external checks. The lead analyst finishes the brief from admitted evidence and carries unresolved questions into the affected assumptions.

Use disclosed contributions to explain changes in sales or profit. Leave any unattributed growth explicit and test whether the case depends on it. Check that the selected demand, pricing, margins and capital needs can occur together in the same case.

## Explain material assumptions

For the few assumptions that could materially change value, funding or the investment call, keep one short decision note:

**Dated business evidence and baseline → operating mechanism → selected magnitude and timing → effect on cash, value and the decision.**

Include the strongest contrary evidence. Distinguish what the evidence supports from the remaining analyst judgment, including a retained forecast. For an improvement, express the required change in simple business or financial terms—for example, the extra annual EBIT needed from a margin uplift—and explain how and when it could occur. Recent growth anchors a continuation case; it does not establish its duration. Directional evidence without an amount or timing stays qualified rather than becoming a precise forecast.

Where support is thin, use an existing case or one relevant sensitivity to show whether a plausible alternative changes the call. State when even the range is judgmental. Identify the next observation that would change the view; missing optional disclosure alone does not require waiting. Cash reconciliation establishes arithmetic, not the reliability of its assumptions, and average cash generation does not establish funding at a payment date.

When comparing runs, distinguish new evidence, changed interpretation and discretionary choices such as exit multiples or capital allocation. Hold other assumptions fixed to isolate an effect, or show the main contributions and disclose that several choices changed together. A clearer narrative alone does not justify a different number.

Completion: each material assumption has an explained amount and horizon or an explicit gap, with its consequence for the decision. Use the existing notes and report sections; no extra model or exhaustive table is required.

## Turn a judgment into an evidence test

For a material claim, record: **observation and source → economic mechanism → financial consequence → strongest contrary evidence**. Keep the observation separate from the interpretation. Missing disclosure means the claim is unproven; it does not establish that the company lacks the attribute.

| Judgment | Working meaning and useful evidence | What is insufficient or could weaken it |
|---|---|---|
| Earnings and cash driver | An operating variable with a material causal effect on sales, costs or cash tied up in the business. Identify the exposed activity and direction of effect. Examples: units sold, realized price/mix, capacity utilization, labor productivity, inventory or customer collection terms. | “Growth” and “margin expansion” merely restate outcomes unless their causes are explained. Do not count utilization in both a higher margin and a second profit uplift. |
| Pricing power | Ability to obtain better prices or terms while preserving demand/customer economics and the company's profit over a relevant period. Look for realized prices alongside volumes, retention, customer alternatives, cost changes and margin development. | Inflation pass-through, FX, a richer product mix, temporary shortages or an announced increase alone do not establish durable pricing power. Lost volumes/share or later discounts are counterevidence. Separate these effects when disclosed; otherwise qualify the claim. |
| Sustainable competitive advantage | A specific mechanism that makes attractive economics hard to displace: switching costs, cost position, distribution, trusted product performance or another evidenced barrier. Look for customer behavior, replacement alternatives, competitor entry and returns through a cycle. | High margins, growth, an acquisition strategy or management's “market leader” label alone do not establish the mechanism or its duration. Falling retention, new substitutes or the cost of defending the position can weaken it. |
| Operating leverage | A change in activity produces a disproportionate profit change because some costs do not rise equally. Compare compatible revenue/EBIT changes and explain fixed versus variable costs, utilization, mix and capacity limits. | A high incremental EBIT margin can reflect a small denominator, an acquisition, FX, one-off costs or price pass-through. Neither a recovery quarter nor a gross-margin/EBIT-margin gap establishes sustainable scalability. |
| Management credibility | Important, dated commitments compared with actual results on the same scope, plus explanations and subsequent decisions. Use a small promise/outcome record for material guidance, investment projects or acquisitions. | A miss alone does not establish dishonesty; a target met through changed definitions does not establish delivery. Separate controllable decisions, external shocks, revised scope and unavailable outcomes. |
| Capital allocation | What owners receive from cash reinvested, acquisitions, debt choices, dividends and buybacks, after the capital and funding required. Examine subsequent operating/cash results, purchase cost and per-share consequences where disclosed. | EPS accretion alone can hide expensive funding, acquisition adjustments or dilution. High group growth does not establish attractive returns on incremental capital. Incomplete attribution remains a qualification, not an invented acquisition IRR. |
| Cash-backed earnings | Profit supports recurring cash after working capital, investment and financing/lease needs on an explicit ownership basis. Use the adopted cash bridge and several periods where possible. | A temporary inventory release, stretched suppliers, tax timing or SBC addback can flatter cash conversion. Group cash is not automatically available to parent-common shareholders. |
| Prospective dilution | A plausible change in common shares/claims from awards, convertibles, issuance and transaction funding. Show the starting share basis, relevant terms and forecast treatment. | Historical diluted EPS shares are not target-date shares. A repurchase authorization is not an executed offset; cash used for buybacks must also enter the cash/debt bridge. Keep SBC expense and ownership effects distinct without charging the same award twice. |

Keep only the judgments material to the company. Do not require an exhaustive evidence table in the human memo; put a short explanation beside the assumption and source detail in the appendix.

## Check management delivery and insider evidence

Both checks are required in the existing management section. They are evidence and judgment checks, not new scores or valuation formulas. A retrospective supplement may complete them without rewriting a historical report or changing its saved financial outputs; identify the exact report and frozen cutoff it supplements.

When a frozen [management history](../../../management-history.md) is supplied, use its explicit five-year window and source scope as an evidence index. Check the underlying report pages and paginate the captured transaction dataset yourself. Distinguish source-owner groupings from corroborated people/entities, actual observation dates from publication dates, and role intervals from employment years. Unknown identities/dates stay unknown; aliases and name similarity do not force joins. Challenge omissions and correction candidates while retaining every captured row, including entities and outside-window context. Treat prior supplements and curated associations as derived research to verify. Derive your own forecast assumptions and confidence consequences. Include a compact, sourced management-history table in the readable memo: current people/roles, material dated handovers, ownership measurement dates and relevant promise/transaction links. Keep the complete row ledger in supporting notes, with unresolved identities and dates visible.

**Commitments and outcomes.** Search the available annual and interim history for the few material promises that bear on the thesis: financial guidance, operational projects, acquisitions, investment or cash delivery. Keep a compact record in existing notes or supporting report detail:

- Original wording and source/page; publication date or explicitly unknown date; management or responsible body when disclosed. A later restatement does not establish when a target was first introduced.
- Metric, accounting definition, business/ownership scope and original deadline. Distinguish a standing policy or medium-term ambition from a dated commitment.
- Revisions, changed definitions and explanations, each with its source and date; preserve the original basis rather than substituting the latest target.
- Later comparable outcomes and their periods/sources. Mark delivered, missed, pending, withdrawn, not comparable or unverified only as the evidence supports. Show material acquisition, FX or perimeter differences; an immature deadline is not a miss, and a rounded value may not establish a strict threshold.
- What the record supports about delivery and candour, the strongest contrary evidence and the consequence for confidence in the material forecast assumptions. Separate management decisions from external shocks; a miss alone does not establish dishonesty.

Cover the available years where material rather than repeating the current target against the latest quarter. If no relevant commitment can be established, identify the reports/periods searched and the remaining gap. Do not invent publication dates, responsibility, a project return or a categorical trust verdict.

**Insider evidence.** Identify the captured dataset, capture/check date, analysis cutoff, transaction-date range and number of relevant rows reviewed. Page through the relevant captured rows to exhaustion, recording coverage or any gap. Dataset ordering is not necessarily trade-date ordering; a first page cannot establish the latest activity. Keep transaction and verification dates distinct and exclude information not admitted at the cutoff.

Preserve row/source identifiers and original versions. Check correction or duplicate candidates before treating rows as separate events; if the capture supplies no reliable event/correction link, state that limit and do not silently deduplicate or add repeated rows together. Establish transaction-type definitions where evidence permits, and inspect incentive/other flags and terms. Separate discretionary purchases or sales from awards, exercises, transfers, related-company transactions and unknown categories. An unknown code or a blank flag is not proof of an open-market trade. Do not net unmatched categories or double-count a multi-leg incentive event. A zero/empty dataset establishes only the captured coverage, not no trading.

Summarize the material dated observations, their size relative to disclosed personal ownership when comparable evidence exists, and whether they change confidence in alignment. Personal wealth, motives and complete current holdings may be unknown. Clustered buying may reflect a common incentive program; selling can have several explanations. Use the [checked Hedborg passage](../../../research/hedborg-podcast-evidence.md#checked-passages) as interpretation guidance, not as evidence for the issuer. State when transactions are context only and why. Keep register/issuer verification and unavailable terms explicit; do not fetch new evidence inside a frozen analyst run or make trades a mechanical signal.

## Select three to five drivers

Choose the smallest set that explains earnings and cash. The following are possible drivers, not mandatory sector templates:

| Business | Possible operating chain | Cash/capital question |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturer | Demand/orders → units shipped; price/mix and utilization/productivity → profit per unit. | Can existing capacity serve demand? What investment, inventory and receivables accompany growth? |
| Recurring software | Customers × recurring revenue per customer, with retention and new sales explaining the change; development/support/sales costs explain EBIT. | Are development costs expensed or capitalized? How do billing terms, acquisitions and share awards affect cash and per-share outcomes? |
| Retailer/distributor | Transactions/units × basket or realized price; sourcing, mix and store/logistics costs explain EBIT. | Does growth require more stores, leases and inventory? Are collections and supplier terms sustainable? |

Use one supported operating equation for valuation: normally revenue × EBIT margin, or the existing volume × unit-profit equation when appropriate. Use the business chain to justify its inputs rather than adding overlapping models. Where a useful driver is not disclosed, state the proxy and what it cannot show.

## From market evidence to scenarios

The [combined market-and-peer role](../../betaforge-market-peer-research/SKILL.md) can provide external evidence. The lead analyst still owns the assumptions: a peer average or industry projection is not the target company's forecast. Identify product/geography exposure, demand, price/mix, costs, capacity and required capital together. State why the particular company could gain or lose share, or differ from the market.

Build bear/base/bull as **coherent business conditions**, not percentage changes from the share price. Bear should test a material adverse mechanism; base is the selected central operating outcome; bull needs evidence for the favorable conditions. They are representative cases, not automatically statistical quantiles or a complete distribution. Do not make every bear a growing business by convention, and do not force losses where its economics do not support them.

### Fictional example

All figures are invented to illustrate the process. They are not recommended forecast bands. A manufacturer has latest annual sales of 1,000 and EBIT of 100, in currency millions, with a compatible historical margin range of 8–12%.

| Same three-year horizon | Operating conditions to investigate | Revenue | EBIT margin | EBIT | Cash consequence to investigate |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Bear | Customers cut orders; underused plants and discounting reduce profit. | 950 | 7% | 66.50 | Lower profit and slow inventory clearance can constrain dividends or require borrowing; a revenue decline does not guarantee cash release. |
| Base | Demand recovers moderately; existing capacity supports sales and a modest productivity gain. | 1,150 | 10.5% | 120.75 | Growth requires funded inventory/receivables and recurring capex before cash can reduce debt. |
| Bull | Stronger demand and product mix support higher sales and profitability at the historical high. | 1,300 | 12% | 156.00 | Verify capacity and incremental investment: stronger EBIT need not produce equivalent distributable cash. |

`EBIT = revenue × margin` explains the arithmetic, not why the forecasts are likely. Each row still needs evidence for its magnitude and combined conditions. A margin below the historical range needs explanation just as a new high does. Complete the earnings-to-cash and common-equity bridges, justify the multiple and forecast shares, and only then compare values and dividend-inclusive returns with the dated share price. Changing that price alone must not silently change the operating assumptions or target value.

Assign probabilities only when comparable outcome frequencies or a documented elicitation support the numbers. Record the evidence, uncertainty and omitted outcomes. Otherwise leave the cases unweighted and explain the gap. More peer data can improve the operating argument without establishing scenario probabilities.

## Basis and limits

These definitions operationalize [policy: philosophy, evidence, quality and valuation](../../../analysis-policy.md), rather than adding an investment strategy. The preserved [investor-writing index](../../../research/investor-writing-examples.md#primary-source-examples) supplies useful explanatory patterns: Buffett's 2007 capital-needs examples, Munger's shareholder perspective and Pershing Square's operating chains.
