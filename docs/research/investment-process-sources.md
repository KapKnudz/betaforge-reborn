# Primary sources for Betaforge’s investment process

Reviewed 7 November 2026. The interviews support several principles in the [analysis policy](../analysis-policy.md). Betaforge’s scenario method, cash definitions and return hurdle remain project decisions. The [Hedborg draft](../planning/investment-philosophy-draft.md) is the supplied synthesis. Its episode index identifies recordings for checking the remaining attributions.

## Petter Hedborg

- **Case horizon and peak margin.** In Redeye’s written Q&A, Hedborg describes a quick two-to-three-year analysis using a peak EBIT margin and an exit multiple. In the same answer he says the business may take longer to reach that margin and explains why the size of his positions limits short-term trading. He also says he looks five to ten years out for his largest holdings. For Betaforge, use the EBIT margin supported by the end of the forecast horizon. Explain separately what the business could earn over a longer period and the milestones needed to reach that margin. A higher exit multiple needs its own rationale. [Redeye, 9 December 2024, pp. 81–82, answers on philosophy and selling](https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/rdey-cms-prod/app/uploads/2024/12/micro-cap-theme-report-q3-20242.pdf#page=81)
- **Read the issuer and explain the recovery.** Hedborg tells Affärsvärlden that he reads reports and releases himself, focuses on the business and growth drivers, and assigns each case a horizon. His advice to beginners about avoiding unprofitable businesses specifically cites the risk of an equity raise. In Redeye he recalls buying CellaVision after two loss-making quarters because earlier profits and his understanding of the sales cycle supported a recovery. This is his retrospective account. For Betaforge, check the latest compatible results against prior profitable periods and show what would distinguish delayed sales from lost demand. Model financing and dilution if cash runs short before recovery. [Affärsvärlden, 6 December 2019, points 1–4](https://www.affarsvarlden.se/artikel/borsfallor-for-nykomlingar-6981022), [Redeye, p. 83, CellaVision Q&A](https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/rdey-cms-prod/app/uploads/2024/12/micro-cap-theme-report-q3-20242.pdf#page=83)
- **Use management’s record.** Hedborg describes asking how a licensing arrangement pays and why particular activities succeed or fail. He flags missed promises, borrowing for product development and a CEO who glosses over trouble. For Betaforge, compare a material promise with the dated outcome and trace any miss to the operating or cash assumption it affects. The answers support this check. A fixed management score or universal founder preference would require separate evidence. [Redeye, pp. 82–83, answers on management discussions and red flags](https://s3-eu-west-1.amazonaws.com/rdey-cms-prod/app/uploads/2024/12/micro-cap-theme-report-q3-20242.pdf#page=82)

## John Armitage

- **Cash and the price paid.** In the publisher’s transcript of *Behind the Balance Sheet* episode 1, Armitage bases value on cash flows and says he wants a path to conventional valuation support within a few years. In the Tesla discussion he treats share-based compensation as a cost and considers cash generation without EV credits. He also looks for executives who admit mistakes and is wary of accounting that flatters results to justify pay. The publisher labels its transcript AI-produced and lightly edited. For Betaforge, reconcile forecast EBIT to cash after working capital and recurring investment. Show the compensation expense, cash-flow addback and expected dilution separately so their effects are counted once. The cash measure and equity bridge follow project policy. [Behind the Balance Sheet, 19 August 2021, “Transcript,” management exchange, valuation discussion and closing advice](https://behindthebalancesheet.com/podcasts-singles/podcast-episode-1/#:~:text=AI-produced%20and%20only%20lightly%20edited)
- **Business quality and valuation.** Egerton publicly describes fundamental research into quality companies at attractive prices and says it invests primarily in liquid global large caps. That is a firm statement. The transferable check is to ask what growth and cash the purchase price requires, then test a plausible operating disappointment. Norges Bank Investment Management’s 7 October 2026 episode description identifies pricing power and sell decisions as interview topics. The description alone provides no basis for attributing particular cash, ROIC or valuation rules to Armitage. [Egerton, “Our Objective” and “Investment Philosophy”](https://www.egertoncapital.com/#:~:text=Investment%20Philosophy), [Norges Bank Investment Management episode](https://www.nbim.no/en/news-and-insights/podcast/2026/john-armitage-how-he-invests-building-egerton-and-what-worries-him-about-ai/)
