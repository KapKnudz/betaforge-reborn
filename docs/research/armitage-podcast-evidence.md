## Checked passages

- **Price increases have to survive competition and costs.** Armitage explains that a company with pricing power can raise prices despite competition, and that margins can improve when realized increases persist above cost inflation. Check volumes, mix or retention where available. A list price increase can coexist with lower realized prices or lost sales. [In Good Company, about 4:58–6:02](https://www.nbim.no/en/news-and-insights/podcast/2026/john-armitage-how-he-invests-building-egerton-and-what-worries-him-about-ai/)
- **Follow the end market.** Armitage recalls noticing that some European growth companies drew their growth from emerging markets. He cites his exposure to the beer business as a route into research on Brazil. Interbrew and Ambev formed InBev in 2004. For Betaforge, trace sales or earnings to the regions that drive them before choosing market evidence and peers. [Value Investing with Legends, about 2:04–2:50](https://valueinvestingwithlegends.libsyn.com/navigating-macroeconomic-shifts-with-john-armitage), [AB InBev’s transaction history](https://www.ab-inbev.com/about-us/our-heritage)
- **Use incremental margin to size a miss.** Armitage uses the profit earned on incremental sales to explain why a small revenue shortfall can cause a large profit miss. Start with comparable changes in EBIT and sales. Check which costs, prices and volumes moved before using the result in a downside case. [In Good Company, about 15:02–16:10](https://www.nbim.no/en/news-and-insights/podcast/2026/john-armitage-how-he-invests-building-egerton-and-what-worries-him-about-ai/), [Betaforge’s calculation definitions](../calculations.md)
- **Check profit against the balance sheet.** Armitage looks at inventory, receivables, payables and cash, including whether factoring receivables boosted the cash figure. If cash conversion improves, explain whether collections actually improved, working capital was released or financing changed the timing of receipts. [In Good Company, about 16:10–17:34](https://www.nbim.no/en/news-and-insights/podcast/2026/john-armitage-how-he-invests-building-egerton-and-what-worries-him-about-ai/)

## Named cases

**Airbus.** Armitage starts with units sold and price. Individual contract prices are private, so he asks when backlog pricing reaches revenue, how much profit an extra delivery contributes, and what R&D and exchange rates do to the result. Airbus reported 793 commercial aircraft deliveries in 2025 and a year-end backlog of 8,754. It attributed higher adjusted commercial aircraft EBIT to deliveries, the hedge rate and lower R&D, partly offset by tariffs. Reported EBIT for the segment fell. Use deliveries and mix to anchor aircraft sales, account for services separately where material, and test a range for realized pricing on a consistent EBIT basis. [In Good Company, about 11:35–12:32](https://www.nbim.no/en/news-and-insights/podcast/2026/john-armitage-how-he-invests-building-egerton-and-what-worries-him-about-ai/), [Airbus FY 2025 results](https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-02-airbus-reports-full-year-fy-2025-results)

**Altice.** Armitage recalls selling after a slight quarterly miss even though it barely affected his medium-term model. The combination of weak sales growth, cost cuts, acquisitions and debt left little room for a disappointment. He gives no date, security or quarter to reconstruct the trade. A useful test is to carry a modest operating miss through to cash after investment and debt service, then show the remaining room to fund the business. [In Good Company, about 20:02–21:29](https://www.nbim.no/en/news-and-insights/podcast/2026/john-armitage-how-he-invests-building-egerton-and-what-worries-him-about-ai/)

**Ryanair.** Armitage says weak pricing caught him off guard and weighs the signal against the medium-term outlook when asked about monthly traffic data. In a separate Q1 FY25 release, Ryanair reported 10% more passengers, 15% lower average fares, 11% higher operating costs and a 46% drop in profit after tax, all year over year. It also cited Easter timing. Armitage did not specify which quarter he meant. The report illustrates why a demand or pricing case should track units, realized price and costs separately. [Behind the Balance Sheet #37, about 32:45–33:29](https://behindthebalancesheet.com/podcasts-singles/37-the-analyst/), [Ryanair Q1 FY25 results, 22 July 2024](https://corporate.ryanair.com/news/ryanair-reports-q1-profits-down-46to-e360m-as-traffic-grows-10-to-55-5m-at-15-lower-air-fares/)
